# Mrs Pell's Impossible Shelves

**Difficulty** 6/7 (hard) &nbsp;&nbsp; **Grid** 5 x 5 &nbsp;&nbsp; **Clues** 14 &nbsp;&nbsp; **id** `2546e1b0803f`

## Introduction

Mrs Pell's secondhand bookshop shelves by a system she alone understands, and she has gone to Margate for a fortnight.
Five volumes are involved. Determine each volume's shelf, buyer, keepsake and price.
- **Volume**: a book of sea shanties, a water-stained atlas, a Latin primer, a treatise on clocks and a guide to fungi.
- **Shelf** (listed from lower to higher): the bottom shelf, the second shelf, the third shelf, the fourth shelf and the fifth shelf.
- **Buyer**: Rodrigo, Sybilla, Augustus, Ignatius and Fenella.
- **Found inside**: a recipe for quince jelly, a postcard from Hull, a pressed violet, a tram ticket and a pawn ticket.
- **Pencilled price**: seven and six; half a crown; ninepence; four shillings; and a guinea.
Every volume has a different shelf, buyer, keepsake and price, so within each category every item is used exactly once.

## Clues

1. The volume that was bought by Ignatius does not sit on the fifth shelf. The volume that contains a pressed violet and the fifth shelf are exactly three shelves apart in shelf order.
2. The volume that is priced at half a crown is exactly one shelf lower than the second shelf, with no shelf in between. A book of sea shanties does not sit on the second shelf.
3. The volume that contains a tram ticket is lower than the fifth shelf in shelf order.
4. At least one of the following holds, and possibly more than one: (a) The volume that was bought by Augustus is priced at seven and six. (b) The volume that contains a recipe for quince jelly does not sit on the fifth shelf.
5. The volume that was bought by Augustus falls somewhere between a book of sea shanties and the fourth shelf in shelf order.
6. The third shelf falls somewhere between the volume that is priced at half a crown and the volume that is priced at ninepence in shelf order.
7. The volume that is priced at seven and six and the fourth shelf are exactly one shelf apart in shelf order, in one direction or the other.
8. The volume that was bought by Rodrigo falls somewhere between a treatise on clocks and the fifth shelf in shelf order.
9. The volume that was bought by Fenella and the fourth shelf both sit at odd-numbered shelves or both at even-numbered shelves.
10. A water-stained atlas is exactly one shelf lower than the volume that contains a recipe for quince jelly, with no shelf in between.
11. The volume that contains a postcard from Hull and the volume that is priced at four shillings are exactly two shelves apart in shelf order.
12. The volume that contains a postcard from Hull and the volume that was bought by Rodrigo are exactly three shelves apart in shelf order.
13. A Latin primer does not contain a tram ticket.
14. A guide to fungi and the volume that was bought by Rodrigo are exactly one shelf apart in shelf order, in one direction or the other.
