## Introduction

The Ravelston Chess Club ladder is recalculated monthly and disputed continuously.
Five players are involved. Determine each player's placing, opening, clock and habit.
- **Player**: Yevgenia, Dermot, Hieronyma, Birgitta and Casimir.
- **Placing** (listed from better to worse): first, second, third, fourth and fifth.
- **Favourite opening**: the Grunfeld, the Vienna Game, the London System, the Catalan and the Dutch Defence.
- **Clock**: a borrowed digital clock, no clock at all, a phone app, a wooden analogue clock and a hotel alarm clock.
- **Table habit**: rearranging the captured pieces, narrating in a whisper, humming, leaving between moves and removing their shoes.
Every player has a different placing, opening, clock and habit, so within each category every item is used exactly once.

## Clues

1. Yevgenia falls somewhere between fifth and first in placing order. Dermot falls somewhere between fifth and third in placing order.
2. If (a) holds then (b) also holds, but not necessarily the reverse: (a) The player who uses a borrowed digital clock does not place first. (b) The player who uses a wooden analogue clock and third are exactly one place apart in placing order, in one direction or the other.
3. The following two do NOT both hold; either one alone may hold, and so may neither: (a) The player who is known for leaving between moves and first are exactly one place apart in placing order, in one direction or the other. (b) The player who is known for leaving between moves and Hieronyma are exactly one place apart in placing order, in one direction or the other.
4. The player who is known for rearranging the captured pieces and fifth are exactly one place apart in placing order, in one direction or the other.
5. One of the player who favours the Dutch Defence and first sits at an odd-numbered placing and the other at an even-numbered placing.
6. The player who favours the Catalan is better than fourth in placing order.
7. Birgitta is exactly one place better than the player who uses a wooden analogue clock, with no placing in between.
8. At least one of the following holds, and possibly more than one: (a) Birgitta is better than the player who favours the Grunfeld in placing order. (b) The player who is known for leaving between moves is exactly one place better than the player who favours the Grunfeld, with no placing in between.
9. The following two do NOT both hold; either one alone may hold, and so may neither: (a) Birgitta is better than the player who favours the Grunfeld in placing order. (b) The player who is known for rearranging the captured pieces and Dermot both sit at odd-numbered places or both at even-numbered places.
10. The player who uses a hotel alarm clock falls somewhere between the player who favours the Dutch Defence and the player who favours the London System in placing order.
11. At least one of the following holds, and possibly more than one: (a) The player who is known for narrating in a whisper is exactly one place better than the player who uses a wooden analogue clock, with no placing in between. (b) The player who is known for leaving between moves does not favour the London System.
12. Hieronyma is not known for humming.
13. The player who is known for removing their shoes is exactly one place better than Dermot, with no placing in between.
14. The player who uses a phone app is better than the player who is known for humming in placing order.
