## Introduction

Down the board, judged on the floor as much as on the clock, and the wool classer is taking notes.
Five shearers are involved. Determine each shearer's placing, breed, handpiece and penalty.
- **Shearer**: Manaia, Bevan Toft, Deirdre Pollok, Shona Garrick and Rangi.
- **Placing** (listed from better to worse): first, second, third, fourth and fifth.
- **Breed**: a Coopworth, a Merino, a Corriedale, a Romney and a Polwarth.
- **Handpiece**: Lister blades, a wide comb, a borrowed handpiece, an old Sunbeam and a narrow comb.
- **Penalty**: a slow pen, a dropped sheep, tufts on the britch, second cuts and a skin nick.
Every shearer has a different placing, breed, handpiece and penalty, so within each category every item is used exactly once.

## Clues

1. The shearer who shears a Polwarth and second are exactly three places apart in placing order.
2. At least one of the following holds, and possibly more than one: (a) The shearer who is docked for a dropped sheep is worse than second in placing order. (b) One of the shearer who uses an old Sunbeam and the shearer who is docked for a skin nick sits at an odd-numbered placing and the other at an even-numbered placing.
3. At least one of the following holds, and possibly more than one: (a) Rangi and fifth are exactly one place apart in placing order, in one direction or the other. (b) One of the shearer who shears a Corriedale and Shona Garrick sits at an odd-numbered placing and the other at an even-numbered placing.
4. The shearer who is docked for tufts on the britch and fifth are exactly two places apart in placing order.
5. At least one of the following holds, and possibly more than one: (a) The shearer who shears a Romney and Rangi are exactly three places apart in placing order. (b) The shearer who shears a Polwarth places third.
6. The shearer who is docked for second cuts is exactly one place better than the shearer who uses a narrow comb, with no placing in between.
7. The shearer who is docked for a slow pen and Deirdre Pollok are exactly one place apart in placing order, in one direction or the other.
8. The shearer who shears a Merino and the shearer who is docked for a skin nick are exactly three places apart in placing order.
9. Manaia falls somewhere between the shearer who uses a borrowed handpiece and the shearer who uses a narrow comb in placing order.
10. The shearer who shears a Coopworth and the shearer who is docked for a skin nick are exactly four places apart in placing order.
11. The shearer who shears a Merino and the shearer who is docked for a slow pen are exactly one place apart in placing order, in one direction or the other.
12. The following is NOT true: The shearer who uses an old Sunbeam and the shearer who is docked for a skin nick are exactly one place apart in placing order, in one direction or the other.
13. The shearer who shears a Merino is exactly one place better than the shearer who uses a wide comb, with no placing in between.
